AI + Design Studio / Stop-motion research

Artificial
Motion

How far can generative AI be pushed to create convincing stop-motion animation, and what can its successes and failures reveal about the technology's creative potential and limitations?

I’m working toward a 30–60 second short. I judge each shot by its movement and timing, the consistency of its materials, and how much control I have over the next pose.

Experiment log

  1. Day 5 · Flow

    Walk, then wave

    The LEGO figure kept its rigid shape, but its legs bent incorrectly and its head twisted during the wave. On the next pass I specified which joints could move.

  2. Day 6 · Flow

    Clay in motion

    The blob changed shape, but the movement felt computer-generated. I want to try fewer poses and give each change in silhouette a purpose.

  3. 17 Nov 2026 · Review

    What the Flow tests taught me

    A convincing still can fall apart in motion. The bird barely lifted off the ground, and the run cycle missed the obstacle jump even after prompt revisions. I’m keeping these clips for comparison.

Next experiment

Animate the same paper cutout taking off in two ways: generate a clip, then build the action one pose at a time. Compare when its feet leave the ground and whether the paper edges hold their shape.
