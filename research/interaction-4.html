Making paper move

AI Paper Cutout Animation: How We Made a Full Short
Jordan, Cofounder of Framesail AI (2026)
Online article / AI animation workflow and case study

Why I chose this source

This article explores how AI can create paper cutout animation while maintaining consistent textures, layered materials, and character designs across multiple shots. It is relevant to my project because it introduces an alternative to clay and LEGO animation that might be easier for AI to replicate convincingly, while offering techniques for preserving a handmade appearance in AI-generated videos.

What I checked

I wanted to verify the article's claim that paper cutout animation can still look authentic with smooth, computer-generated movement, unlike traditional stop-motion. I compared this with information from South Park Studios (South Park Studios FAQ), which confirms that the show switched from physical paper cutouts to digital animation while maintaining its handmade appearance. This supports the idea that paper cutout animation could work well with AI video generators, although it doesn't prove that AI can consistently recreate the style. Since Framesail is also promoting its own product, I would still need to test whether these results can be reproduced using other AI tools.

How this shaped my project

This interaction helped me understand that there might be better ways to approach AI-generated stop-motion animation than simply asking a video model to recreate traditional stop-motion. The Framesail article introduced paper cutout animation as another possibility, which I hadn't seriously considered before. Compared to the LEGO and clay experiments I've done with Google Flow, paper cutout animation seems like it might be easier for AI to replicate because the characters have simpler movements and don't require as much realistic articulation.

AI helped me recognize that the handmade appearance of stop-motion doesn't necessarily require physical materials. Looking at examples like South Park, I found that digital animation can still maintain the appearance of traditional cutout animation. However, I also had to consider that Framesail is promoting its own product, so its examples might not accurately represent what other AI tools can consistently produce.

This hasn't completely changed my project direction, but it has given me another technique worth exploring. My goal is still to create an AI-generated stop-motion short that demonstrates both the possibilities and limitations of the technology. Rather than continuing to force AI to recreate complicated physical movements, I want to experiment with styles that might work better with its current capabilities.

My next step is to test paper cutout animation using an AI video generator and compare the results with my previous Google Flow experiments. I want to see whether simplifying the animation style actually produces something more convincing, or whether the same problems with movement and consistency still appear.
